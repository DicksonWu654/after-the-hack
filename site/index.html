    Methods and data

    How the numbers were built

    Scope. The headline set is hacks and exploits with a published USD loss, , totalling in reported losses (tracker-reported USD at the time of each incident). It merges DeFiLlama, SlowMist, Rekt, DeFiHackLabs and researcher lists, de-duplicated. Chainalysis and CertiK publish larger totals because their scope adds categories such as phishing of individuals, most rug pulls and the LuBian outlier.

    Recovery. Documented recovery through any channel, valued at the theft-date price where one exists (the definitions list the exceptions) and capped at each incident's loss, over all . Pledges and payments by victims or parent companies to make users whole are tracked separately from recovered funds. of hacks of $1M or more have not yet been searched for an outcome and count as unrecovered, so count-based recovery rates are floors. Company claims (Bybit, KuCoin) are included and flagged; with Bybit at its independently corroborated freeze the any-channel rate is .

    Returns and negotiation. Return rates come from an earlier on-chain messages study of large exploits and key compromises, with bounds of to depending on how unresolved cases behave. Message texts were decoded from public RPC calldata and compared against the datasets' copies: of 48 checked rows, 16 matched exactly and 21 differed only in line breaks, spacing, case or truncation (each card says which, and every quoted passage is an exact substring of the decoded message; the other 11 rows mismatched or could not be found, so they are left unquoted); 42 of the 43 EVM transactions were confirmed on two endpoints. Sender roles come from dataset coding and message pairing, and each card says which.

    Attribution and arrests. North Korea attribution is tiered: government (FBI, DOJ, OFAC, UN monitors, Korean and Japanese police) and firm (Chainalysis, TRM, Elliptic, ZachXBT, victim forensics). Arrest counts are lower bounds from public records searched for most of the largest 150 incidents ( of the top 100 have not been searched) and a sibling case list; "none found" marks hacks where the sources searched turned up no public record. Where a dollar figure in a table is the master dataset's and differs from the text, the table says so.

    The dot field and look-up box. Each dot is one of the headline incidents. Arrested or charged means a person was arrested or charged for the hack itself. Recorded returns means the data records some of the money as returned; the outcome line names who returned it only where the data does. Other recoveries means the data records some recovered funds that were not returned: freezes, seizures, halts, rescues or an unspecified channel. North Korea: government means a government attributes the hack to North Korea. The look-up card also shows firm attributions and researcher-list leads, which are not counted as attributions. An outcome line never prints an amount as recovered when its evidence is unverified.

    Attack anatomy. The attack chapter uses a separate, narrower set: EVM exploits that someone reproduced as a DeFiHackLabs proof of concept, with the attack transaction, attacker contract, funder and deployment time read from chain data. Its shares are by count within that set, which is made up of contract exploits. Monthly record counts come from DeFiLlama and SlowMist. AI claims are graded verified, credible claim or speculation from the primary page, and only verified items are described as findings.
