        How this was built

        AI-assisted research; sources, uncertainty and corrections are documented below.

        The data covers hacks and exploits with a published USD loss, , totalling in reported losses (tracker-reported USD at the time of each incident). It joins DeFiLlama, SlowMist, rekt.news and DeFiHackLabs with curated outcome layers, and the largest 150 hacks were re-checked in a separate pass. Recoveries are valued at the theft-date price where one exists and capped at each hack's loss.

        Read the full method in the paper. Code and the available supporting files live in the After the Hack repository. Corrections are welcome there as issues.

        Definitions

        Reported
        A press, firm or company statement. Most loss and recovery figures are reported.
        Verified
        A primary record read for this project: a decoded chain transaction, a court docket, a DOJ release or an FBI notice.
        Recovered
        Money returned, frozen, seized, halted, rescued or recovered by another route. Payments the victim made to its own users are left out of every recovery figure.
        Returned
        Handed back by the attacker or an insider. The look-up names who returned it only where the data does.
        Other recorded recovery
        Frozen by a token issuer, exchange or protocol; seized by police or a court; stopped by a chain halt, validator freeze, fork or rollback; rescued; handed back by someone other than the attacker; or recovered by an unspecified route.
        No recorded recovery
        No recovery was found in the sources checked, or the outcome was never checked. hacks of $1M or more were never checked, so their outcome is unknown.
        Arrested or charged
        A person arrested or charged for the hack itself. People charged only for laundering the money are counted separately. Arrests were searched for the largest hacks and a list of known cases, so the count is a floor.
        North Korea, government
        A government attributes the hack to North Korea. Firm attributions and researcher-list leads are tracked separately and shown as tags in the look-up.
        "No ... found"
        A search result. It means no public record turned up in the sources searched.
