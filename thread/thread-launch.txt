I dug into every public record I could find of crypto hacks since 2016: what happened to the money, the negotiations, the arrests. 1,377 hacks, $16.5B stolen. I'm open sourcing all of it, and here's what I found.
---
About 19% of stolen dollars has come back by any route. 10.0% was returned by the attacker or an insider, 9.0% was frozen, seized or halted, and 81.0% has no documented recovery.

Without Poly Network, Liquid and KuCoin it falls to 13.2%.
---
By dollars, compromised keys and infrastructure lose the most (35.0%), ahead of smart-contract logic (24.8%).

By count, contract bugs lead: 35.7% of incidents vs 15.7% for keys and infra. Oracle manipulation and access control also show up more by count than by dollars.
---
The most common way money comes back is that the attacker hands it over: 52.5% of the $3.14B recovered.

Issuer, exchange or protocol freezes are 19.1%, law-enforcement seizures 10.5%, chain halts 8.3%. About 1 in 8 large exploits ends with a voluntary return.
---
Freezes work on tokens someone controls. Tether froze $181k in the $1.46B Bybit theft, 0.012% of the loss.

Across all routes about $43M (2.9%) of Bybit is corroborated as frozen. Issuer freezes in other hacks caught 1% to 11%.
---
Chains can step in too. Sui validators froze 72.6% of Cetus's $223M. Cronos spotted its attack in ~36 min, halted and rolled back, restoring 92.4%.

Median time to recovery: issuer freezes 1.5 days, exchanges 3 days, law-enforcement seizures 779 days (169 and 1,389 days).
---
10% is the going rate. The median opening bounty offer has been 10% every year since 2021, and 102 of 181 on-chain offers were exactly 10%.

Attackers who settled kept a median 10% (n=35). At the far end, Verus kept 25% and Mango 41%.
---
Shezmu offered 10% and the exploiter countered on-chain:

"Sure we can talk about the refund, but only considering 20% as bounty."

Five minutes later the team replied: "20% is a deal."
---
Euler took a harder line on-chain: "If 90% of the funds are not returned within 24 hours, tomorrow we will launch a $1M reward for information that leads to your arrest"

5 days 19 hours later the attacker answered: "No intention of keeping what is not ours."
---
Teams that reached out saw money come back in 36.5% of known cases (80 of 219). Without outreach it was 3.5% (9 of 256).

Offer size, deadlines and legal threats made no measurable difference. The attackers willing to give money back are the ones who answer.
---
When money does come back, it's quick. Median 2.4 days from the hack to the first return, and 21 of 23 documented returns landed inside a week, timed by block timestamps. The other two took 8 and 22 days.
---
9 of the 100 biggest hacks, about 1 in 10, have led to someone being charged for the hack itself.

Across all 1,377 hacks it's 16, about 1 in 86 (1.2%).
---
The wait is long: a median of 14.5 months (439.5 days) from hack to first arrest or charge, across the 14 cases with a dated first action. 10 of 14 took over a year.

Two fast cases, Platypus (6 days) and Mango (76), involved a KYC'd exchange or a public identity.
---
Of 23 people charged over a hack or its laundering: 8 convicted, pleaded guilty, or plea agreement on file; 8 charged but not in custody, 3 pending, 2 acquitted, 1 unknown, 1 vacated, appeal pending.

On Oct 7 a jury convicted Spalletta on all counts over Uranium Finance hacks.
---
Government attribution puts North Korea behind 22.6% of stolen dollars, from 2.3% of incidents (32 incidents). In 2025 it was 58.9% of dollars, mostly Bybit.

Adding firm and researcher attributions it's 39.1% of dollars and 6.9% of incidents (95 incidents).
---
North Korean hacks almost never come back: 8.3% of dollars recovered across 95 incidents (government or firm), 3.8% without KuCoin and Munchables. Everything else: 25.8%.

We found none in custody for an exchange or bridge theft in sources searched through 21 Oct 2026.
---
Attackers now run the whole exploit inside the attack contract's own deployment transaction far more often: 0 of 95 in 2020-22, 14.3% in 2024, 40.5% in 2025, 34.1% in 2026 so far.

By dollars it's 8% to 25% since 2024, so it's mostly small and mid-size exploits.
---
On AI: 28 claims logged, 0 verified as an AI-written exploit in a real hack.

In simulation, AI agents reproduced 207 of 405 past exploits (Anthropic's SCONE-bench). Recorded exploits rose from 12 to 38 a month (2025 vs Mar to Sep 2026): more attacks, more reporting, or both.
---
Liquid Network, $320M. The attacker returned 3,400 BTC about 26 hours after the drain and held back 598.5 BTC, writing on-chain:

"You SHALL pay 10% using your own money as bug bounty"

Blockstream: "It is not white-hat activity. It is theft." Unresolved as of 21 Oct.
---
NEAR Intents lost about $3.8M. NEAR told the attacker publicly "We have identified you, sir." and set a 48-hour deadline.

100% came back within about 36 hours of detection, with no bounty reported.
