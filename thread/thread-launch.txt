I went through the public records on 1,377 crypto hacks since 2016 to see what happens after the money's gone: the negotiations, the freezes, the arrests.

$16.5B stolen. Only 16 are known to have led to an arrest or charge.

I'm open sourcing all of it 🧵

---

The short version: 19% of stolen dollars has a documented recovery.

10% from attackers handing it back. 9% from freezes, seizures, rollbacks and other routes.

The other 81% has no recorded recovery.

---

Start with the famous ones.

Poly Network lost $611M in 2021. The attacker gave back about $578M, and Tether froze another $33M.

Nobody has been charged for it that I could find.

---

Euler lost $197M in 2023 and threatened a $1M reward for info leading to the attacker's arrest and the return of all funds.

The attacker returned the funds and wrote on-chain: "I'm sorry."

---

Bybit is the biggest in my data: $1.46B in Feb 2025. The FBI tied it to North Korea.

Tether froze $181k of it. That's about 0.01%.

---

Ronin lost $624M in 2022, also tied to North Korea by the FBI. At least $30M was seized.

Government sources tie 22.6% of all stolen dollars to North Korea. Only 3.4% of that has been recovered or frozen.

---

The FTX hack, hours after it filed for bankruptcy: US prosecutors charged three people over a SIM-swap theft linked to it.

One pleaded guilty and another has a plea agreement on file.

---

About half the money that comes back is handed back by the attacker, often after a public on-chain negotiation.

The usual opening offer is a 10% bounty, the median every year since 2021.

Shezmu's attacker asked for 20%. Five minutes later the team replied: "20% is a deal."

---

Arrests are rare. Just 9 of the 100 biggest hacks led to someone being arrested or charged for the hack.

When someone does get caught, the first action against them comes a median of about 14 months after the hack.

---

Court cases cut both ways. On Oct 7 a jury convicted Uranium Finance's exploiter. A judge overturned the Mango trader's convictions, and the US appealed.

---

Sometimes the chain itself steps in. Sui validators froze $162M stolen from Cetus, and Cronos rolled back its chain to restore $111.2M.

---

What about AI? Across 28 public claims, I found zero verified AI-written exploits in real hacks. It does show up in phishing and tooling.
